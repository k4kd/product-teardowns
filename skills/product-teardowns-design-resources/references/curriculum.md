# Design curriculum

These tracks are the path to mastery in product, UX and UI design. For each concept, pick one **Start** (intuition first, under an hour), one **Deep** (a book, guide or long-form course) and one **Mastery** resource (the primary source: the spec, the original book, the research) from the matching track. Every link here returned HTTP 200 on 2026-10-19, and each is by the author or institution named.

Within each track, resources are listed in learning order. Check what each resource assumes before giving it, and give it only if the design ledger or the doc already covers those foundations.

The bar is the same as the other curricula: written by people who did the work or defined the standard, dense, and still worth it in five years. No "10 UI tips" listicles, no Dribbble-style inspiration feeds.

## Interaction design and usability
*Why it matters:* a product people can't figure out doesn't get a second chance.
- Start: [Jakob Nielsen, "10 Usability Heuristics" (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), the checklist every design review starts from
- Deep: [Jon Yablonski, *Laws of UX*](https://lawsofux.com/): Fitts, Hick, Jakob, Miller and the rest, each with its research
- Mastery: [Don Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/don-norman/the-design-of-everyday-things/9780465050659/): affordances, signifiers, mapping, feedback and conceptual models, from the person who named them

## Interaction design craft
*Why it matters:* knowing the heuristics isn't the same as designing well. This is the craft of turning goals into flows.
- Start: [NN/g articles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/), research-backed guidance by topic (forms, navigation, errors)
- Deep: [Cooper, Reimann, Cronin & Noessel, *About Face* (4th ed., Wiley)](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/About+Face%3A+The+Essentials+of+Interaction+Design%2C+4th+Edition-p-9781118766576), goal-directed design, personas and interaction principles
- Mastery: [Don Norman's essays and books](https://jnd.org/books/), human-centred design beyond the first book

## Visual hierarchy and UI craft
*Why it matters:* hierarchy decides what people see first, and so what they do.
- Start: [Erik Kennedy, "7 Rules for Creating Gorgeous UI"](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html), light, contrast, spacing and alignment, explained for non-designers
- Deep: [Wathan & Schoger, *Refactoring UI*](https://www.refactoringui.com/), hierarchy, spacing, colour and depth, with before-and-after examples
- Mastery: [Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/), a complete visual system with its reasoning (layout, elevation, colour roles)

## Typography
*Why it matters:* most of an interface is text. Type decides whether it's read or skimmed past.
- Start: [Matthew Butterick, *Practical Typography*](https://practicaltypography.com/) ("Typography in ten minutes" first), free online
- Deep: [Google Fonts Knowledge](https://fonts.google.com/knowledge), type anatomy, pairing, scales and variable fonts
- Mastery: [Butterick, *Practical Typography*, the full book](https://practicaltypography.com/): line length, spacing and type systems in depth

## Colour and contrast
*Why it matters:* colour carries meaning and hierarchy, and bad contrast locks people out.
- Start: [Stripe, "Designing accessible color systems"](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems), building a palette that passes contrast by design
- Deep: [Refactoring UI, the colour chapters](https://www.refactoringui.com/), shades, greys and colour roles in practice
- Mastery: [Josef Albers, *Interaction of Color* (Yale)](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300179354/interaction-of-color/), how colours change each other, from the Bauhaus teacher who studied it

## Accessibility
*Why it matters:* about one in six people lives with a disability, and accessible design is better design for everyone.
- Start: [W3C WAI, "Introduction to Web Accessibility"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/fundamentals/accessibility-intro/)
- Deep: [W3C, "Understanding WCAG 2.2"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/), the intent and examples behind each success criterion
- Mastery: [W3C, *Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2*](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), the standard itself

## Design systems
*Why it matters:* a system is what keeps 200 screens consistent, and lets a team ship fast without breaking the look.
- Start: [Brad Frost, *Atomic Design*, chapter 2](https://atomicdesign.bradfrost.com/chapter-2/): atoms, molecules, organisms, templates and pages
- Deep: [Brad Frost, *Atomic Design*](https://atomicdesign.bradfrost.com/) (free online), making and maintaining a system
- Mastery: [Material Design 3, "Design tokens"](https://m3.material.io/foundations/design-tokens/overview), how decisions become tokens that code and design share

## Platform conventions
*Why it matters:* people bring habits from every other app on their phone. Fighting them costs you.
- Start: [Apple, *Human Interface Guidelines*](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines)
- Deep: [Google, *Material Design 3*](https://m3.material.io/)
- Mastery: both guides in depth for the platform you ship on, read with the [NN/g articles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/) on mobile usability

## Content design and microcopy
*Why it matters:* the words on a button often decide whether it's pressed.
- Start: [GOV.UK, "Content design: planning, writing and managing content"](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/content-design), from the team that defined the discipline
- Deep: [Sarah Richards, Content Design London](https://contentdesign.london/) (Richards created content design at GOV.UK)
- Mastery: [Kinneret Yifrah, *Microcopy: The Complete Guide*](https://www.microcopybook.com/)

## Motion and feedback
*Why it matters:* motion explains what just happened. Done badly, it slows everything down.
- Start: [Material Design 3, "Motion"](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/overview), durations, easing and purpose
- Deep: [Josh Comeau, "An Interactive Guide to CSS Transitions"](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/animation/css-transitions/)
- Mastery: [Val Head](https://valhead.com/), *Designing Interface Animation* and her talks on purposeful motion

## Performance as UX
*Why it matters:* a fast product feels better designed. Users judge speed in tenths of a second.
- Start: [Jakob Nielsen, "Response Times: The 3 Important Limits" (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/): 0.1 s, 1 s and 10 s
- Deep: [web.dev, "Web Vitals"](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) (Google), measuring loading, responsiveness and stability
- Mastery: [web.dev, "The RAIL model"](https://web.dev/articles/rail), budgets for response, animation, idle and load

## Research and critique
*Why it matters:* you are not your user. Research and critique are how a team sees past its own taste.
- Start: [Jakob Nielsen, "Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users" (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/)
- Deep: [NN/g, "Design Critiques"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-critiques/), running critiques tied to goals, not taste
- Mastery: [Steve Portigal](https://portigal.com/), *Interviewing Users*, the standard book on research interviews

## Trust and dark patterns
*Why it matters:* manipulative design wins a metric this quarter and loses the customer's trust for good, and regulators now fine it.
- Start: [Harry Brignull, deceptive.design, "Types"](https://www.deceptive.design/types), the catalogue of deceptive patterns, by the person who coined the term
- Deep: [Harry Brignull, *Deceptive Patterns* (book)](https://www.deceptive.design/book)
- Mastery: [Mathur et al., "Dark Patterns at Scale" (Princeton, 2019)](https://webtransparency.cs.princeton.edu/dark-patterns/), a crawl of 11,000 shopping sites and what it found
