# Product management curriculum

These tracks are the path to mastery in product management. For each concept, pick one **Start** (intuition first, under an hour), one **Deep** (a book, course or long-form essay) and one **Mastery** resource (the primary source: the original book, paper or essay that defined the idea) from the matching track. Every link here returned HTTP 200 on 2026-10-19, and each is by the author or institution named. Some are paywalled in part, and that is noted.

Within each track, resources are listed in learning order. Check what each resource assumes before giving it, and give it only if the product ledger or the doc already covers those foundations.

The bar is the same as for the technical curriculum (Karpathy's Zero to Hero): written by someone who did the work, dense, and still worth it in five years. No listicles, no content-marketing blogs, no unsourced "growth hacks".

## Product sense and discovery
*Why it matters:* most products fail because they solve a problem nobody has badly enough. Discovery is how you find out before you build.
- Start: [Teresa Torres, "Opportunity Solution Trees"](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/), the visual map from a goal to the problems worth solving
- Deep: [Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*](https://www.producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits/), the weekly interviewing and assumption-testing practice
- Mastery: [Marty Cagan, *Inspired* (2nd ed.)](https://www.svpg.com/books/inspired-how-to-create-tech-products-customers-love-2nd-edition/), how the best product teams discover and deliver

## Users and jobs to be done
*Why it matters:* people don't buy products; they "hire" them to make progress in a situation. Knowing the job explains who you really compete with.
- Start: [Christensen, Hall, Dillon & Duncan, "Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done" (HBR, 2016)](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done)
- Deep: [Bob Moesta on Lenny's Podcast, "The ultimate guide to JTBD"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-ultimate-guide-to-jtbd-bob-moesta), how switch interviews uncover the job (Moesta co-developed the theory)
- Mastery: [Christensen, Cook & Hall, "Marketing Malpractice" (HBR, 2005)](https://hbr.org/2005/12/marketing-malpractice-the-cause-and-the-cure), the original argument for segmenting by job, not by customer type

## Strategy and moats
*Why it matters:* a good product without a moat gets copied. Strategy is the reason you'll still be winning in five years.
- Start: [Ben Thompson, "Aggregation Theory" (Stratechery)](https://stratechery.com/2015/aggregation-theory/), why the internet rewards whoever owns the user relationship
- Deep: [Hamilton Helmer, *7 Powers*](https://7powers.com/), the seven sources of durable advantage (scale economies, network effects, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, process power)
- Mastery: [Michael Porter, "What Is Strategy?" (HBR, 1996)](https://hbr.org/1996/11/what-is-strategy), why operational excellence isn't strategy and trade-offs are

## Positioning and go-to-market
*Why it matters:* the same product sells or flops depending on what people compare it to.
- Start: [April Dunford](https://www.aprildunford.com/), positioning essays and talks from the person who wrote the method
- Deep: [April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*](https://www.aprildunford.com/obviously-awesome), the 10-step positioning process
- Mastery: [Brian Balfour, "Four Fits"](https://brianbalfour.com/four-fits-growth-framework), market, product, channel and model have to fit each other (Balfour led growth at HubSpot and founded Reforge)

## Growth loops and channels
*Why it matters:* funnels leak, and loops compound. The best products grow because using them brings in the next user.
- Start: [Reforge, "Growth Loops are the New Funnels"](https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops) (Balfour, Winters, Chen and Verna)
- Deep: [Andrew Chen's essays](https://andrewchen.com/), a decade of primary writing on viral loops, retention and marketplaces (Chen led growth at Uber and is an a16z partner)
- Mastery: [Andrew Chen, *The Cold Start Problem*](https://www.coldstart.com/), how networks start, tip and defend themselves, with company-by-company cases

## Network effects and marketplaces
*Why it matters:* network effects are the strongest moat in tech, and the hardest to start.
- Start: [NFX, "The Network Effects Manual"](https://www.nfx.com/post/network-effects-manual), 16 types, with examples
- Deep: [Andrew Chen, *The Cold Start Problem*](https://www.coldstart.com/), the atomic network, the tipping point and the ceiling
- Mastery: [Eisenmann, Parker & Van Alstyne, "Strategies for Two-Sided Markets" (HBR, 2006)](https://hbr.org/2006/10/strategies-for-two-sided-markets), the subsidy side, pricing and winner-take-all dynamics

## Activation and habits
*Why it matters:* most users leave in the first session. Activation is getting them to value before they do.
- Start: [Lenny Rachitsky, "How to determine your activation metric"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-determine-your-activation) (partly paywalled)
- Deep: [Nir Eyal, *Hooked*](https://www.nirandfar.com/hooked/), the trigger, action, variable reward, investment loop
- Mastery: [BJ Fogg, the Fogg Behavior Model](https://behaviormodel.org/): behaviour happens when motivation, ability and a prompt meet (Stanford Behavior Design Lab)

## Retention and product-market fit
*Why it matters:* retention is the truest sign that a product works. Everything else is borrowed growth.
- Start: [Lenny Rachitsky, "What is good retention"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29), benchmarks by category from 20+ operators
- Deep: [Andrew Chen, "Losing 80% of mobile users is normal"](https://andrewchen.com/new-data-shows-why-losing-80-of-your-mobile-users-is-normal-and-that-the-best-apps-do-much-better/), retention curves and what the best apps do differently
- Mastery: [Sequoia Arc, "The PMF Framework"](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/pmf-framework/), three archetypes of product-market fit and what each demands

## Monetisation and pricing
*Why it matters:* pricing is the fastest lever on revenue and the least studied.
- Start: [Madhavan Ramanujam on Lenny's Podcast, "The art and science of pricing"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-art-and-science-of-pricing-madhavan)
- Deep: [Ramanujam & Tacke, *Monetizing Innovation* (Wiley)](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Monetizing+Innovation%3A+How+Smart+Companies+Design+the+Product+Around+the+Price-p-9781119240860), design the product around the price, and test willingness to pay first
- Mastery: [Nagle & Müller, *The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing* (Routledge)](https://www.routledge.com/The-Strategy-and-Tactics-of-Pricing-A-Guide-to-Growing-More-Profitably/Nagle-Muller/p/book/9781032016825), the standard text on value-based pricing

## Metrics and unit economics
*Why it matters:* a product team that can't read its numbers can't tell a win from noise.
- Start: [a16z, "16 Startup Metrics"](https://a16z.com/16-startup-metrics/), the definitions people get wrong (bookings vs revenue, CAC, churn)
- Deep: [David Skok, "SaaS Metrics 2.0"](https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/saas-metrics-2/), LTV, CAC payback and the SaaS P&L, from a five-time founder turned VC
- Mastery: [Croll & Yoskovitz, *Lean Analytics*](https://leananalyticsbook.com/), the one metric that matters for each business model and stage

## Experimentation
*Why it matters:* most ideas don't work. Kohavi reports that only about a third of ideas tested at Microsoft improved the metric they aimed at, so intuition alone loses.
- Start: [Kohavi & Thomke, "The Surprising Power of Online Experiments" (HBR, 2017)](https://hbr.org/2017/09/the-surprising-power-of-online-experiments)
- Deep: [Kohavi, Tang & Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments*](https://experimentguide.com/), the practitioner's bible (OEC, guardrails, pitfalls)
- Mastery: [Kohavi et al., "Rules of Thumb" papers](https://exp-platform.com/rules-of-thumb/), lessons from tens of thousands of experiments

## Estimation and forecasting
*Why it matters:* every product decision is a bet on numbers you don't have yet. Rough, honest ranges beat confident guesses.
- Start: [Douglas Hubbard, *How to Measure Anything*](https://www.howtomeasureanything.com/): anything can be measured well enough to decide, and calibration can be trained
- Deep: [Hubbard's book and free calibration resources](https://www.howtomeasureanything.com/), decomposition, ranges and the value of information
- Mastery: [Tetlock & Gardner, *Superforecasting*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/227815/superforecasting-by-philip-e-tetlock-and-dan-gardner/), what the best forecasters do, from the research that measured them

## Execution and decisions
*Why it matters:* strategy is worthless if the team builds the wrong thing in the right way.
- Start: [Gary Klein, "Performing a Project Premortem" (HBR, 2007)](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem), imagine it failed and ask why, before you start
- Deep: [Marty Cagan, *Empowered*](https://www.svpg.com/books/empowered-ordinary-people-extraordinary-products/), teams given problems to solve, not features to build
- Mastery: [Bryar & Carr, *Working Backwards*](https://www.workingbackwards.com/), Amazon's PR/FAQ and narrative-memo decision process, from two long-time Amazon leaders

## AI products
*Why it matters:* AI changes what's cheap to build, what users trust and what fails silently.
- Start: [Google PAIR, *People + AI Guidebook*](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook/), designing for uncertain outputs, trust and feedback
- Deep: [Chip Huyen, "Building LLM applications for production"](https://huyenchip.com/2023/04/11/llm-engineering.html), the real costs and failure modes
- Mastery: [Chip Huyen, *AI Engineering*](https://huyenchip.com/books/), evaluation, data and system design for foundation-model products
